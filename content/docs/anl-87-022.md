ANALYSIS ANL-87-022                 TOP SECRET // WICKER BASIN // NOFORN
DIRECTORATE P, WITH [[x:10]] LABORATORY (DOE)
SUBJECT:  LAMINA ANALYSIS, K-01 (ROOM B-2, HELD IN SITU)
DATE:     22 JUN 1987

1. Three cores were taken through the floor of Room B-2 at the door, the middle and the far wall. Room B-2 has been held in situ under lease since 1963 and has not been entered with the door closed since 27 OCT 1962. Each core shows, from the top: a surface course approx. 1 mm thick; a single lamina approx. 2 mm thick; the original slab. The floor stands approx. 3 mm above its 1962 level. Over the area of the room this is approx. 40 litres: the volume of a body of 35 to 45 kg.

2. The surface course is cement and fine aggregate. Its hardness (rebound number 37) matches the surrounding slab to within two points. It contains no tissue. It is not a coating. It is the only part of the floor that is floor material. Its lower face merges into the lamina over about 0.5 mm.

3. The lamina is a sheet of four components. There is no cement in it. (a) Bone, finely broken, both cortical and trabecular. Fragments number in the hundreds of thousands per core and range from 0.1 mm to 3 mm. Fracture surfaces are fresh, with no sign of healing or weathering. (b) Soft tissue, flattened to a dark brown leathery film about 0.2 mm thick that binds the fragments. Skin, muscle and fat can be told apart under the microscope. (c) Hair: strands of long dark brown head hair, 4 to 38 cm, with the roots attached. (d) Dried body fluid (blood, serum, fat), which fills the spaces between the fragments and stains everything brown. The lamina is dry and firm. Nothing in it has decomposed and it has no smell. Fragments are present across the whole floor, at the same density at each core point. Their size falls with distance from the door: the coarsest lie at the threshold, the finest at the far wall. The long axes of the fragments, and the hair, lie parallel to the floor, in a pattern that radiates from the door. Unfused growth plates and tooth-crown fragments give an age at death of 10 to 13 years. The lamina is bonded to the slab along its lower face. The structure is the same as that of each of the 31 laminae of K-31 ([[ANL-85-016]]).

4. DNA was extracted from bone, tissue and hair roots. The lamina is from one female individual.

5. A reference sample from R. D. PRUITT (shelter warden, INC-62-001) was obtained in 1987 under medical pretext (Q-1).

6. RESULT: The lamina individual is a child of R. D. PRUITT. Probability of paternity greater than 0.999.

7. R. D. PRUITT has no children. County birth records, school enrollment 1956-62 and the 1960 census record no child of PRUITT. The 1960 census enumeration sheet for the PRUITT household lists PRUITT, R. D. (line 14) and PRUITT, M. (line 15). Line 16 is blank. The next household begins at line 17.

8. ON THE PROCESS. The 1962 witnesses heard cracking and grinding for several seconds. The findings are those of a sound of that kind. The body was not dissolved and did not flow. The skeleton was broken by pressure into fragments of progressively smaller size, and the fragments were driven outward from the door across the floor. Soft tissue and hair were squeezed out with them and lie between them, and the fluid with them. The coarse-to-fine gradient says that she entered at the door and that the force travelled from there. The fragments' long axes say that it pressed down and outward, like a roller.

The force acted evenly over the whole floor, is attributable to no visible object, and left no marks on the concrete. The surface course is not part of the lamina. It has the composition and hardness of the original slab. Either the surface was reformed after the lamina was laid, or the lamina was driven in beneath it. We cannot tell which. In either case the floor took her in and then closed over her. The witnesses' account of a soft, warm floor, and the hard floor found on 31 OCT, are consistent with a floor that finished itself.

This is not the passive addition of mass assumed in ANL-80-031 (H3). It is a forcible assimilation, and the room is the agent.

9. RECOMMENDATION: No further contact with PRUITT.
