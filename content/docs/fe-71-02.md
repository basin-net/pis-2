1. PURPOSE
To determine whether a K-room placed in an ordinary civilian setting will produce events, how often, and whether those events can be detected from outside by J-6 Gap Analysis alone.

2. METHOD
A community hall was built with federal assistance channelled through [[x:17]] (cover). Its main room measures 36 ft 0 in by 33 ft 6 in (1,206.000 sq ft; K-21, occupancy 120). The room has one double door and no opening windows; ventilation is mechanical, specified "for noise abatement". The fire marshal's posted occupancy is 150. The floor is poured terrazzo, sealed.

3. DETECTION
No missing-person report was filed after any event. J-6 Gap Analysis detected each event within 9 to 40 days, from: school enrollment gaps; utility accounts in the name of no one; mortgage payments from an account with no holder; a pastor's request to the county for "grief counselling for the whole congregation". Witnesses also report a loud noise from the floor, lasting about ten seconds, after the doors were closed for the toast or the grace: "cracking, then grinding". The sound was assumed to come from the building. No one could say what had made it. J-6 Q-1 interviews: 61. See [[TR-71-117]].

4. FINDING
SUCCESSFUL. A K-room in a civilian setting produces events at a rate set by attendance. The events conceal themselves (D0). Gap Analysis detects them reliably after the fact.

5. DISPOSITION
Hall retained under [[directives|Directive 72-03]]; floor area unaltered. The floor had risen approx. 9/16 in. in total, and patrons began to trip at the doors. It was ground down and resurfaced APR 1974. The grinding dust was white. It was collected and removed by J-3. Under raking light the whole floor shows a finer grain than terrazzo of its age. In public use.
