1. (TS//WB) SUMMARY
Certain enclosed rooms impose a maximum occupancy. Such rooms are identified by floor area alone. When the last door of such a room is closed while the number of persons inside exceeds its occupancy, the persons who entered last, in the number of the excess, undergo LAMINATION. They are not remembered.

2. (TS//WB) CONDITIONS
a. The room must be closed. An open door, window or hatch of any size prevents the effect.
b. The floor area must equal a listed value (a "K-value") to within approximately 0.5 square inch. Shape, use, furnishing and construction are irrelevant. Thermal movement of the walls is enough to bring a room into or out of value; several known rooms are "live" only within a band of 3 to 4 degrees F.
c. Persons are counted at the moment the last door latches. The effect follows at once.
d. Order of entry is set by the moment the person's last point of contact with the floor outside the room is broken. A person who leaves and re-enters is counted as entering at re-entry.
e. Living human beings are counted. Corpses and animals are not. Anesthetized and comatose persons are counted. Persons in whom brain death has been determined are not counted (EXP-67-008). The phenomenon counts as a clerk would, not as a physician would.

3. (TS//WB) LAMINATION
Over 6 to 9 seconds the subject is forced into the floor. The process is violent and it is loud. It opens with a rapid series of dry fractures. These are followed by a wet grinding, as of gravel worked into mortar, which rises to a peak at about the fourth second and falls away. The sound carries through masonry; in ordinary buildings it has been heard two floors away. Where there are several subjects their processes overlap and the sound lasts longer: about 10 seconds for 11 subjects ([[FE-71-02]]), about 20 seconds for 31 ([[INC-85-007]]).

The body is not dissolved and does not flow. The skeleton does not yield: it is broken, by a pressure with no visible source and no tool marks, into fragments that grow smaller as they are driven outward from the point of entry across the whole floor. Soft tissue, hair and body fluid are extruded with them. Subjects are conscious for the first 2 to 3 seconds. Film shows every conscious subject crying out until about the third second. No witness has reported hearing a voice.

At the end of the process the floor is warm (approx. 37 C), soft and slightly elastic. It stays so for about six hours, then hardens. Hardening is complete within 30 hours. The hardened surface takes the character of the original floor: concrete becomes concrete, terrazzo terrazzo, wood plank with grain, carpet a rigid carpet that rings like stone. By hardness, finish and wear it cannot be told from the floor it replaced. It contains no tissue. Only this uppermost surface becomes floor. Beneath it each subject remains a separate layer of what the subject was, which dries and firms but does not become floor, and does not fuse with the layer above or below.

Clothing and carried objects are not affected. They fall where the subject stood.

The floor is raised by the volume of the subject divided by the floor area: approx. 5 mm per subject in a room of 150 sq ft. A core taken through the floor shows the original surface material on top, and beneath it one lamina per subject, in order of entry. Each lamina is a sheet of finely fragmented bone, flattened soft tissue, hair and dried body fluid. The laminae lie flat on one another like the pages of a closed book. The boundaries are sharp and nothing crosses them. Nothing in a lamina decomposes.

The floor does not rise above approx. 1/32 of the height of the ceiling (3 in. under a ceiling of 8 ft). When a lamination would take it higher, the laminae already in the floor are compressed, silently and without loss, and the new lamina is laid at full thickness on top. Compressed laminae remain separate and can still be counted. The value is the height at which persons who use a room first sense that it has changed. The step at the door is not taken into account, and that is where a raised floor is noticed. See [[ANL-79-014]].

4. (TS//WB) WHAT IS NOT REMEMBERED
Every record that identifies the subject is revised to exclude the subject, retroactively and without any exception known to this office. Personal memory is revised in the same way. Revision is not perfect. See section 5.

5. (TS//WB) RESIDUALS
- Witnesses remember a loud sound from the floor and cannot say what made it. The sound is remembered. Its cause is not.
- The subject's voice is not remembered, though witnesses stood within feet of it.
- Numbered lists skip. Typed forms keep space for an entry that is not there.
- Paper that bore the subject's name in ink keeps the pen impression but not the ink.
- Photographs and film keep the subject's image. No viewer can identify the subject.
- Objects the subject made remain. The attribution is blank.
- Witnesses report SHORTFALL: unease, grief without an object, habitual actions on behalf of a person they cannot name (a place set at a table; a door left unlocked at night).
- Children under approximately seven years keep fragments, usually reported as an imaginary companion.
- Domestic animals continue to wait for the subject.

6. (TS//WB) COUNTING
Because every record that names a subject is lost, this office knows only numbers. All PA-001 work is conducted under Blind Tally (numbered tokens, no names). All program personnel are counted each morning. See [[directives|Directive 64-01]].

8. (TS//WB) NOTES ON VALUES
- For 24 of 31 known values the occupancy is close to one person per 10 sq ft. The others are lower, some much lower. Two are zero.
- No K-value has been found outside the range 61 to 1,206 sq ft. SURVEYOR Pass C (700 to 2,000 sq ft, 40 subjects) found none.
- J-2 estimates that not fewer than 150 values remain undiscovered. See [[ANL-80-031]].
- Incidence in the USSR, where construction is metric, is approximately 1/40 of U.S. incidence (J-2 Foreign Programs; Soviet term UPLOTNENIE, "crowding-in"). Within Japan, every known event has occurred on a U.S. installation.
- No new value has been recorded since K-31. See [[ANL-86-011]].
