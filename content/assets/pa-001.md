1. (TS//WB) SUMMARY
Certain enclosed rooms impose a maximum occupancy. Such rooms are identified by floor area alone. When the last door of such a room is closed while the number of persons inside exceeds its occupancy, the persons who entered last, in the number of the excess, undergo LAMINATION. They are not remembered.

2. (TS//WB) CONDITIONS
a. The room must be closed. An open door, window or hatch of any size prevents the effect.
b. The floor area must equal a listed value (a "K-value") to within approximately 0.5 square inch. Shape, use, furnishing and construction are irrelevant. Thermal movement of the walls is enough to bring a room into or out of value; several known rooms are "live" only within a band of 3 to 4 degrees F.
c. Persons are counted at the moment the last door latches.
d. Order of entry is set by the moment the person's last point of contact with the floor outside the room is broken. A person who leaves and re-enters is counted as entering at re-entry.
e. Living human beings are counted. Corpses and animals are not. Anesthetized and comatose persons are counted. Persons in whom brain death has been determined are not counted (EXP-67-008). The phenomenon counts as a clerk would, not as a physician would.

3. (TS//WB) LAMINATION
Over 6 to 9 seconds the subject loses structural integrity, spreads evenly across the entire floor and becomes continuous with the floor surface. Skeletal and muscular structure offers no resistance. Subjects have been observed to remain conscious for the first 2 to 3 seconds. The process itself makes no sound. The floor surface stays warm (approx. 37 C) for up to three hours.

Clothing and carried objects are not affected. They fall where the subject stood.

The floor is raised by the volume of the subject divided by the floor area: approx. 5 mm per subject in a room of 150 sq ft. A core taken through the floor shows one lamina per subject, in order.

4. (TS//WB) WHAT IS NOT REMEMBERED
Every record that identifies the subject is revised to exclude the subject, retroactively and without any exception known to this office. Personal memory is revised in the same way. Revision is not perfect. See section 5.

5. (TS//WB) RESIDUALS
- Numbered lists skip. Typed forms keep space for an entry that is not there.
- Paper that bore the subject's name in ink keeps the pen impression but not the ink.
- Photographs and film keep the subject's image. No viewer can identify the subject.
- Objects the subject made remain. The attribution is blank.
- Witnesses report SHORTFALL: unease, grief without an object, habitual actions on behalf of a person they cannot name (a place set at a table; a door left unlocked at night).
- Children under approximately seven years keep fragments, usually reported as an imaginary companion.
- Domestic animals continue to wait for the subject.

6. (TS//WB) COUNTING
Because every record that names a subject is lost, this office knows only numbers. All PA-001 work is conducted under Blind Tally (numbered tokens, no names). All program personnel are counted each morning. See [[directives|Directive 64-01]].

8. (TS//WB) NOTES ON VALUES
- Every known K-value is a whole number of square inches.
- For 24 of 31 known values the occupancy is close to one person per 10 sq ft. The others are lower, some much lower. Two are zero.
- No K-value has been found outside the range 61 to 1,206 sq ft. SURVEYOR Pass C (700 to 2,000 sq ft, 40 subjects) found none.
- J-2 estimates that not fewer than 150 values remain undiscovered. See [[ANL-80-031]].
- Incidence in the USSR, where construction is metric, is approximately 1/40 of U.S. incidence (J-2 Foreign Programs; Soviet term UPLOTNENIE, "crowding-in"). Within Japan, every known event has occurred on a U.S. installation.
- No new value has been recorded since K-31. K-31 could not have been missed before 1985. See [[ANL-86-011]].
